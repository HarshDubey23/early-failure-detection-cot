\section{Introduction}
Language models fail in the middle of a generation: a reasoning step goes wrong, or an answer
drifts away from its source. Most mitigation methods are \emph{surgical}. Process reward models
(PRMs) are trained and benchmarked to find the \emph{first incorrect step} of a reasoning
trace~\cite{lightman2023,uesato2022,processbench}, and verifier-guided search backtracks to it.
Early-warning detectors predict from a partial trace that the answer will be
wrong~\cite{afzal2025,lugoloobi2026}. Retrieval-augmented systems detect a hallucinated sentence and
rewrite or regenerate from there~\cite{gao2023rarr,dhuliawala2024cove}. Surgery is attractive
because it promises to keep the good part of a generation and pay only for the bad part. It rests
on one assumption: a failure is \emph{decided} at an identifiable step, and resampling from just
before that step beats resampling from scratch.

This paper tests that assumption directly, with one quantity throughout. For a trace written by model $M$, let
$v_k$ be $M$'s probability of reaching a correct (or faithful) output from the prefix ending at step
$k$, estimated by sampling $M$'s own continuations. $v_0$ is the from-scratch solve rate, so
\emph{restart} is resampling from $k=0$ and \emph{backtracking} to step $j$ is resampling from
$k=j-1$. A \emph{choke point} is where $v$ collapses; an \emph{error} is where a human annotator says
the reasoning first goes wrong.

The assumption mostly fails. Many failures are decided before the first step, verifiers find the
human label rather than the choke point, and even a free oracle for the choke point gains little
over restart at matched compute, because a failed on-policy prefix is on average worth no more than a
fresh start (Proposition~1 (Appendix~\ref{app:prop})). Restarting itself recovers
\recMinFour--\recMaxFour\% of failed maths solutions within four attempts' compute. A secondary
study on RAG asks whether the same holds for hallucinations: there, plain detect--restart--abstain
matches a choke-point-aware controller at lower cost, under graders whose limits we report
(Section~\ref{sec:grounded}). The question is
old in another guise: randomised search algorithms face the same choice between continuing a run and
restarting it, and restarts are often optimal there~\cite{luby1993,gomes2000}. Language-model decoding
is such a randomised search.

Every claim carries its evidence level (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}): \textsc{pre} (preregistered
before any data), \textsc{exp} (exploratory) or \textsc{conf} (a preregistered test on new data).
Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Where failures are decided.} Early warning is a difficulty signal (question and
model alone: AUROC \aucQG; the first quarter of the reasoning adds \dIncA, n.s., and is at chance
within a problem). From \TotalRollouts\ on-policy continuations with replicated value curves
($r=\QwenSmallAllSplitR$), \QwenSmallAllCapPct--\QwenMathCapPct\% of failures are decided before the
first step, and the labelled error is the choke point in \QwenTwoAllDec--\QwenSmallAllDec\ of the rest
against a null of 0.16--0.20 (\textsc{pre}; \textsc{conf} on Llama-3.1-8B, \LlamaDec).
\item \textbf{ChokeBench v1}, a benchmark and evaluation protocol (\cbN\ failed traces, four generators). Step localisers graded on the human first error \emph{and}
the counterfactual choke point. The best PRM rule finds \cbPrmDropAccHPct\% of human first errors but
\cbPrmDropAccDPct\% of choke points; a localiser trained and tuned for the choke point does no
better (\cbLearnedAccD). The benchmark has predictive validity on held-out halves of the rollouts
and held-out models: within a trace, naming the choke point raises the recovery gain by \valBothD,
and the human label adds \valBothE\ once the choke point is accounted for (\textsc{exp}).
\item \textbf{Why surgery does not pay, proved and measured.} Restarting recovers \recMinFour--\recMaxFour\% of failed
solutions at four attempts and \recMinEight--\recMaxEight\% at eight. At matched compute, PRM-guided
backtracking loses to restart (\LlamaPolPrmDropFourDiff, \textsc{conf}); a free choke-point oracle
gains \cbOracleGainB; and three short propositions explain the pattern: a failed prefix is worth no
more than a fresh start in expectation, its only advantage is a bounded token saving, and a
restart loop's quality is capped by its detector. In RAG, choosing where to resample adds
\rrHFive\ (\textsc{pre}), and hallucinations do not snowball.
\item \textbf{Secondary: a cheap recipe for RAG, with its law.} Detect, restart, abstain
(DRA). A preregistered detect--resample controller
reduces judged hallucination relative to the base model (\rrHZero, $p=\rrHZeroP$; the judge's agreement with people is $\kappa=\evEOneKappa$, and no human rating of outputs exists yet); plain DRA, tested against the base model on the same items, gives \vbDraEOne\ [\vbDraEOneLo, \vbDraEOneHi] (\textsc{exp}), DRA is equivalent to it
(H1), at \costDra$\times$ the base cost (claim repair \costRep$\times$, the choke-point-aware controller CARE \costCare$\times$,
Chain-of-Verification \costCove$\times$). We prove that DRA's residual hallucination rate is the
detector's $P(\text{hallucinated}\mid\text{pass})$ whatever the retry budget, which predicts
\lawH\% $\to$ \lawPi\% [\lawPiLo, \lawPiHi] with human labels, and gives an operating curve down to
\ocUnionPi\% at \ocUnionDraws\ generations per answer; a four-fold retry budget raises coverage by
\invCov\ points while delivered hallucination stays within \invEOne\ points, as predicted (\textsc{exp}).
\item \textbf{A measurement caution} (\textsc{exp}). The gain of a detector-gated loop looks
\robBaseETwoDiff\ under a grader trained like the detector and \robBaseEOneDiff\ (n.s.) under an
independent judge: the gate partly grades itself.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Scope of the claims.} Proposition~1 is conditional: it bounds the per-attempt value of a
resampling point chosen without lookahead, on failed traces sampled from the generator itself. It
does not say restart is always optimal. Rules that look ahead, or shorter attempts
(Proposition~2), can win, and the ProcessBench traces of Sections~\ref{sec:counterfactual}--\ref{sec:chokebench}
are only near on-policy. Empirically, restart beat PRM-guided backtracking on the held-out
Llama-3.1-8B (\textsc{conf}), but restart against backtracking to the human error was not confirmed
(C1: \LlamaBtRestErr\ [\LlamaBtRestErrLo, \LlamaBtRestErrHi]). On exactly on-policy traces of a reasoning model (Section~\ref{sec:rund}), Proposition~1's
prediction holds, but a matched-budget gain through shorter attempts could not be ruled out.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Process supervision.} Step-level supervision outperforms outcome supervision~\cite{lightman2023,uesato2022};
Monte Carlo rollouts can label steps automatically~\cite{wang2024shepherd}, but such labels disagree with human
judgements because a completion can reach the right answer from a wrong step and vice versa, which
Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025lessons} treat as label noise. ProcessBench~\cite{processbench} evaluates
first-error identification. We study the disagreement itself.

\textbf{Predicting failure early.} Models carry information about their own correctness~\cite{kadavath2022,orgad2024},
and hidden states predict CoT success before or during generation~\cite{afzal2025,lugoloobi2026};
conformal methods certify reasoning prefixes~\cite{cheung2026crop}.
Bulut~\cite{bulut2026} shows that such pooled AUROCs are largely explained by problem difficulty and
fall to chance within a problem. We apply the same control to text detectors on human-annotated traces.

\textbf{Which steps matter.} Resampling from prefixes identifies influential sentences~\cite{bogdan2025},
tokens~\cite{lin2025critical,bigelow2025} and the importance of the first step~\cite{liao2025lost}.
Merrill and Srivastava~\cite{merrill2026} localise the point of no return for deceptive behaviour and
name incorrect solutions as future work; recoverability of error states has been studied for
distillation~\cite{jiang2026ocr}. None compares a counterfactual decision point with human first-error
labels. Work on performative CoT~\cite{li2026performative,boppana2026} finds that answers are often
fixed apart from the written reasoning, which our non-fatal errors echo.

\textbf{Correcting and backtracking.} Unaided self-correction rarely helps~\cite{huang2024}, but
models can correct a mistake once told where it is~\cite{tyen2024}, which motivates verifier-guided
search that returns to the first error. Independent resampling from scratch, as in
self-consistency~\cite{wang2023sc}, is the natural baseline for any such scheme; we compare the two
directly. Compute-matched comparisons of verifier-guided search with independent sampling are
known to depend on difficulty and budget~\cite{snell2024}, and trained backtracking beats parallel
best-of-$n$ on deep search trees but not on shallow ones~\cite{qin2025backtrack}. Bulut~\cite{bulut2026} compares
truncated reasoning prefixes with matched-token restarts for two reasoning models and finds that
long prefixes mostly buy the same successes more cheaply; we study \emph{failed} traces, with
expert error labels, four generators and verifier costs charged. Persistent-pool PRM search revisits
discarded prefixes~\cite{tran2026sps}; our results bound what any such revisiting can gain when the
prefix belongs to a failed on-policy trace. Restart strategies for randomised search have a long
theory~\cite{luby1993,gomes2000}; Appendix~\ref{app:prop} gives the analogue for language-model
decoding.

\textbf{Hallucination recovery.} Detect-then-revise systems rewrite unsupported claims~\cite{gao2023rarr}
or verify and regenerate~\cite{dhuliawala2024cove}; token-level detectors such as
LettuceDetect~\cite{lettucedetect}, trained on RAGTruth~\cite{ragtruth}, make the gate cheap.
Hallucinations can snowball when a model commits to an early claim~\cite{zhang2024snowball}; we
test for this with human span labels and find no excess hazard. Resampling-based interpretability
measures the importance of reasoning steps counterfactually~\cite{bogdan2025,macar2025branches};
we apply the same tool to human error labels, verifiers and recovery policies.

\textbf{What is and is not new.} Counterfactual resampling from prefixes~\cite{bogdan2025,macar2025branches,merrill2026}
and matched-budget comparisons of prefixes with restarts~\cite{bulut2026} already exist. Our
contribution is narrower: a comparison of counterfactual decision points with expert first-error
labels, on failed traces of several generators, together with compute-matched tests of the recovery
policies those labels motivate. ChokeBench is an initial resource built from these experiments;
whether its mismatch holds for new models and datasets, and whether it predicts which interventions
work, is for others to test.

\section{Data and Definitions}
\textbf{Data.} ProcessBench~\cite{processbench} contains 3,400 step-segmented solutions to GSM8K,
MATH, OlympiadBench and Omni-MATH problems (2,842 distinct problems) from 12 open models, each with
the index $e$ of the first erroneous step (or none) and whether the final answer is correct. Correct
and incorrect answers are balanced within each tier by construction.

\textbf{Outcome groups.} A trace is \emph{clean} (no annotated error; 1,179), \emph{recovered}
(an error, but a correct answer; \nRecovered) or \emph{fatal} (an error and a wrong answer; \nFatal).
No clean trace has a wrong answer. The \emph{fatality} of first errors is
$P(\text{wrong} \mid \text{error})$.

\textbf{Counterfactual value.} For a trace $T=(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ written by model $M$, let
$v_k = P_M(\text{correct} \mid q, s_{1:k})$, estimated by sampling $m$ continuations of the prefix
from $M$; $v_0$ is $M$'s from-scratch solve rate and $v_n$ is the observed outcome. The
\emph{decisive step} is $d=\arg\max_k (v_{k-1}-v_k)$, and the \emph{point of no return} is
$c=\min\{k : v_j\le\delta\ \forall j\ge k\}$. A failing trace is \emph{capability-limited} if
$v_0\le\delta$, shows \emph{latent commitment} if $c<e$, \emph{commits at the error} if $c=e$, and
has a \emph{recoverable error} if $c>e$.

\textbf{Evidence levels.}\label{sec:evidence} The hypotheses H1--H4 of the counterfactual
experiment (the commitment categories, the decisive step, sample-split backtracking, $\delta=1/8$ with
1/16 and 1/4 as sensitivity) were committed before any rollout. Analyses added after seeing the
Run A/B data (restart vs.\ first error, repeated splitting, the position-matched null) are
\emph{exploratory}. Before the confirmatory run (Run C) we committed a second plan (git commit
\texttt{459ff22}; \texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION\_RUN\_C.md}). It specified:
\begin{itemize}
\item tests C1--C4 on Llama-3.1-8B, a new family whose traces cover \QuestionsLlamaNew\ new questions;
\item measurement checks R1--R3 on three new independent sampling batches;
\item the practical policies and their cost accounting.
\end{itemize}
Run C reached \LlamaN\ of 274 Llama fatal traces within its 11.5-hour limit. Traces were processed
in a random order, and recovered and clean traces were scheduled after the fatal ones, so none
of those were run. All Run C deviations are reported in the text.

\textbf{Statistics.} Detectors are TF-IDF (uni+bigrams) or one-hot features with logistic
regression~\cite{tfidf}, cross-fitted over 5 folds grouped by problem and repeated over 5 seeds.
Intervals are 95\% question-level cluster bootstrap intervals~\cite{field2007} (1,000 resamples);
differences between detectors use a paired bootstrap on the same traces.

\section{Observational Results}\label{sec:obs}
\subsection{Early failure prediction is a difficulty signal}
Table~\ref{tab:difficulty} and Fig.~\ref{fig:difficulty} compare detectors that read the question,
the generator and tier, the reasoning prefix, or combinations. The question alone reaches
\aucQ\ and question plus generator \aucQG\ [\aucQGLo, \aucQGHi]. A detector reading only the first
25\% of the reasoning is \emph{worse} than that (\dPAvsQG, $p=\dPAvsQGP$), and adding the 25\% prefix
to question and generator changes AUROC by \dIncA\ [\dIncALo, \dIncAHi]. The reasoning adds
\dIncB\ at 50\% and \dIncC\ [\dIncCLo, \dIncCHi] at 100\%, consistently across all five seeds.

Within a problem the generator is the only varying factor besides the reasoning: over \wpProblems\
problems with both a failing and a succeeding trace (\wpPairs\ pairs), the 25\% prefix orders pairs
correctly \wpPA\ of the time [\wpPALo, \wpPAHi] and the full prefix \wpPC\ [\wpPCLo, \wpPCHi],
neither better than generator identity alone (\wpG). Early failure prediction on this benchmark is
therefore almost entirely a statement about which problems are hard for which models.

\subsection{One first error in four is not fatal}
Fatality of first errors is \fatAll\ [\fatAllLo, \fatAllHi]: \nonFatalPct\% of annotated first errors
are followed by a correct answer (Fig.~\ref{fig:fatality}). Fatality falls with difficulty, from
\fatGsm\ on GSM8K to \fatOmni\ on Omni-MATH, and is flat in the position of the error
(\fatOnsetMin--\fatOnsetMax\ across onset quartiles; \fatLast\ only when the error is in the final
step). Fatal and recovered errors occur at the same relative position (Mann--Whitney $p=\mwRhoP$,
$r=\mwRhoR$).

Explicit re-checking language after the error is barely more frequent in recovered than in fatal
traces (\markRec\% vs \markFat\%, $p=\markP$), so self-correction does not explain most
recoveries. Human coding of a stratified sample of recovered traces by two independent coders is in
progress (protocol released); Section~\ref{sec:counterfactual} measures the same question without
coders, by forcing the model to answer at every step.

\subsection{Detectors track errors, not commitment}
If a failure detector captured commitment, it would score recovered traces like clean ones. It does
the opposite (Table~\ref{tab:threeway}): trained to predict a wrong answer, the full-prefix detector
separates recovered from clean traces (\twOutRC) but ranks fatal traces \emph{below} recovered ones
(\twOutFR\ [\twOutFRLo, \twOutFRHi]); with question and generator included the ranking is at chance
(\twOutQGFR). A detector trained to find errors inverts more strongly (\twErrFR). Among erroneous
traces, the fatality of the first error is predicted by question and generator (AUROC \fatPredQG),
and the error step, the prefix up to it or the next one or two steps change this by at most
\fatPredMaxInc.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{For practitioners: how to mitigate cheaply.} Spend compute on a cheap, accurate detector and
on restarts, not on finding where the output went wrong. Gate every output with the detector;
restart only what it flags; abstain (or delete the flagged sentences) when the budget runs out.
Proposition~3 makes the trade-off explicit: the detector sets the hallucination rate of what is
delivered, the budget sets how often the system answers, and the resampling point sets neither.
Report the gain with a grader that shares no training data with the detector.

\textbf{For early warning.} Reported AUROCs for early failure prediction should come with a
question-only baseline, a generator baseline, the increment over both, and a within-problem
concordance. Without them, a detector of problem difficulty cannot be told apart from a detector of
reasoning failure. On ProcessBench, text detectors pass this test only after half the chain. The
counterfactual measurement explains why. Between \QwenSmallAllCapPct\% and \QwenMathCapPct\% of
failures are fixed before the first step, so reading the trajectory can add little for them.

\textbf{For process supervision.} Human first-error labels mark where reasoning first goes wrong,
which is the right target for auditing. They are also, more often than chance, where success
collapses: \QwenMathDec--\QwenSmallAllDec\ of open failures, confirmed on a new model family. But using them as restart
points depends on the model. Per attempt it was worse than restarting for three Qwen models and
equal for Llama, and at matched compute it never clearly beat restarting. Verifier-guided
backtracking should therefore always be reported against restart at matched end-to-end compute,
with the verifier's own cost included.

\textbf{What would change the picture.} The in-sample oracle shows apparent headroom: the best
restart point in hindsight succeeds \QwenSmallAllBtOracle--\LlamaBtOracle\ per attempt, against
\QwenMathBtRestart--\QwenTwoAllBtRestart\ for restart. ChokeBench shows that most of it is
selection on noise: chosen and scored on independent halves of the rollouts, the choke point is
worth \cbOracleGain\ per attempt and \cbOracleGainB\ at a 4$\times$ budget, before any verifier
cost. Backtracking can win only where a prefix holds work the model cannot reproduce from scratch,
which is the regime of long, correct-so-far reasoning~\cite{bulut2026} and of deep search
problems~\cite{qin2025backtrack}, not of failed traces at these scales. The released value curves
let anyone test a localiser against that ceiling.

\textbf{For grounded generation.} The math result carries over: the first hallucinated sentence is
not a better resampling point than the beginning, an online recoverability estimate does not
change that, and hallucinations do not snowball. A detector-gated loop does reduce hallucination,
but by how much depends on whether the evaluator resembles the gate. We recommend that recovery
systems report (i) a matched-compute restart baseline, (ii) an ablation that removes the choice of
resampling point, and (iii) an evaluator that shares no training data with the gate.

\section{Limitations and what remains unverified}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coverage.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Four open models of at most 8B parameters, mathematics only.
  \item Llama contributes \LlamaN\ fatal traces. Its recovered and clean traces were not run, so
  Llama has no noise floor, no event study for those groups, and no answer-curve comparison by
  rollout.
  \item Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and larger models were not run.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{On-policy approximation.} ProcessBench re-segmented the original solutions, and the
decoding settings that produced them are unknown. Prefixes are therefore near-, not exactly,
on-policy, and the proposition holds only approximately for them.
\item \textbf{Truncation.} For the larger models, \QwenMathTruncPct\% and \LlamaTruncPct\% of
continuations hit the token cap without an answer. Treating them as missing changes no
conclusion, but it lowers absolute success rates.
\item \textbf{Equivalence.} Equivalence of decisive-step backtracking and restart at $\pm0.05$ is
established only for Qwen2.5-1.5B and the pooled new batches. Elsewhere, including the
preregistered Llama test, it is inconclusive.
\item \textbf{Exploratory results.} The difficulty and fatality analyses, the answer curves, the
shared-verifier accounting and all policy results on the Qwen models are exploratory.
\item \textbf{Human coding.} The coding of recovered traces by two independent human coders has
not been done. The protocol and blinded sheets are released, and no coding result is reported.
\item \textbf{Hallucination recovery.} One generator (Llama-2-7B-chat); the Llama-3.1-8B arm produced
drafts only (GPU quota), so no recovery claim is made for it. Chain-of-Verification ran on 192 of
750 items. The independent judges agree only weakly with people, and no human rating of the
system's outputs exists yet; a blinded 80-pair, two-rater kit is released.
\item \textbf{ChokeBench} is built from the same rollouts as Sections~\ref{sec:counterfactual}--\ref{sec:policies}
and is exploratory; its localisers are fixed rules, not trained models (the learned policy of
Section~\ref{sec:policies} is the trained case).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Errors are not choke points. On failed reasoning traces from four generators, the human-labelled
first error is a better-than-chance but imperfect locator of the step where success actually
collapses; many failures are decided before the first step; reward models track the label rather
than the collapse; and even a free oracle for the collapse buys about three points over restart at
four restarts' budget, as a supermartingale bound says it should. Restart, which recovers
\recMinFour--\recMaxFour\% of failed solutions within four attempts, is the baseline any
localise-and-repair method must beat at matched compute. In a secondary RAG study the same pattern
holds under the graders we have, but those graders agree only weakly with people, and human ratings
of system outputs are the next test. Whether the gap between errors and choke points holds for new
models and datasets is the question ChokeBench is released to answer.

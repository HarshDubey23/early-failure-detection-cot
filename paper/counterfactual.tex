\section{Counterfactual Commitment}\label{sec:counterfactual}
The observational results show that first errors and outcomes come apart. They do not show where a
failing trajectory actually becomes unrecoverable. We measure that directly by resampling.

\subsection{Setup}
\textbf{Generators and traces.} We use four ProcessBench generators whose weights fit on two
16\,GB GPUs, from two model families:
\begin{itemize}
\item Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25}: all their traces
(\QwenSmallAllN\ and \QwenTwoAllN).
\item Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25math}: all fatal and recovered traces with a gold answer,
and 60 clean traces (\QwenMathN\ in total).
\item Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: the fatal traces completed within the time limit of the confirmatory
run (\LlamaN; Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).
\end{itemize}
The Llama traces cover \QuestionsLlama\ questions, \QuestionsLlamaNew\ of which appear in none of the
Qwen traces (\QuestionsUnion\ distinct questions in total). Gold answers come from the source datasets,
matched on problem text, and are compared with \texttt{math-verify}.

\textbf{Rollouts.} For every prefix $s_{1:k}$, $k=0,\dots,n-1$, we sample $m$ continuations from the
trace's own generator with vLLM~\cite{vllm} in float16, at temperature 0.7 and top-$p$ 0.95, and
estimate $v_k$ as the fraction that reach the gold answer. The sample sizes are:
\begin{itemize}
\item the 1.5B models: $m=\QwenSmallAllM$, as four independent sampling batches of 16 with different
seeds;
\item Qwen2.5-Math-7B: $m=\QwenMathM$;
\item Llama: $m=\LlamaM$.
\end{itemize}
Continuations are capped at 1,536 new tokens for the 1.5B models and at 1,024 for the larger
models. At the cap, \QwenMathTruncPct\% (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and \LlamaTruncPct\% (Llama) of
continuations end without an answer. In total we score \TotalRollouts\ continuations. Every
continuation, its answer and its correctness are released.

\textbf{What can and cannot be read off $v_k$.} Under the generating policy, $v_k$ is a martingale.
Curves conditioned on the eventual outcome therefore drift even when no single step matters: for
any fixed $k$ (and any stopping time),
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[v_k \mid \text{fail}] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[v_k(1-v_k)]}{P(\text{fail})} \le v_0,
\label{eq:supermart}
\end{equation}
because $x(1-x)$ is concave (Appendix~\ref{app:prop}). We therefore never interpret a downward drift
of failing curves by itself. We ask whether the drop is \emph{concentrated} at the annotated error,
against a position-matched null and the noise floor of clean traces. We evaluate backtracking
targets with sample splitting.

\subsection{How precise are the value curves?}\label{sec:reliability}
Each $v_k$ is a binomial estimate, and the decisive step $d=\arg\max_k(v_{k-1}-v_k)$ is a noisy
argmax. We quantify both kinds of uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:reliability}).

\textbf{Value curves.} With the four independent batches of the 1.5B models split two against two,
$v_k$ correlates at \QwenSmallAllSplitR\ and \QwenTwoAllSplitR\ across all prefixes (Spearman--Brown
projection to $m=64$: \QwenSmallAllSplitSB\ and \QwenTwoAllSplitSB). The random split-half
correlation is \QwenMathSplitR\ for Qwen2.5-Math-7B ($m=12$) and \LlamaSplitR\ for Llama ($m=16$).

\textbf{Decisive steps.} We draw every $v_k$ from its Jeffreys posterior and recompute $d$
2,000 times. The modal step has posterior probability at least 0.5 in \QwenSmallAllPostModal\%,
\QwenTwoAllPostModal\%, \QwenMathPostModal\% and \LlamaPostModal\% of the not-capability-limited
fatal traces. Independent halves of the continuations pick the same decisive step in
\QwenSmallAllAgree, \QwenTwoAllAgree, \QwenMathAgree\ and \LlamaAgree\ of traces, against
position-matched nulls of \QwenMathAgreeNull--\QwenTwoAllAgreeNull. The posterior probability
that the decisive step is the annotated error is bimodal: for most traces it is either clearly yes
or clearly no. Disagreement between decisive steps and labels is therefore not measurement noise.

\subsection{Where failing trajectories commit}
Table~\ref{tab:commit} classifies every fatal trace at $\delta=1/8$.

\textbf{Many failures are decided before the first step.} \QwenSmallAllCapPct\%, \QwenTwoAllCapPct\%,
\QwenMathCapPct\% and \LlamaCapPct\% of fatal traces are capability-limited: the model solves the
problem from scratch at most one time in eight, so no step of the trace is a choke point. This is
the counterfactual counterpart of Section~4's difficulty result.

\textbf{When the outcome is still open, the annotated error is often where it closes.} Among fatal
traces that are not capability-limited, the first error is the largest drop in $v$ in
\QwenSmallAllDec, \QwenTwoAllDec, \QwenMathDec\ and \LlamaDec\ of traces. The position-matched nulls
are \QwenSmallAllDecNull, \QwenTwoAllDecNull, \QwenMathDecNull\ and \LlamaDecNull. The differences
are \QwenSmallAllDecMinusNull\ [\QwenSmallAllDecMinusNullLo, \QwenSmallAllDecMinusNullHi],
\QwenTwoAllDecMinusNull\ [\QwenTwoAllDecMinusNullLo, \QwenTwoAllDecMinusNullHi],
\QwenMathDecMinusNull\ [\QwenMathDecMinusNullLo, \QwenMathDecMinusNullHi] and
\LlamaDecMinusNull\ [\LlamaDecMinusNullLo, \LlamaDecMinusNullHi]. For Llama this was preregistered
(C3) and is confirmed. The remaining traces show one of two patterns:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{latent commitment}: success has already collapsed before the written error
(\QwenSmallAllLatentNL\%, \QwenTwoAllLatentNL\%, \QwenMathLatentNL\% and \LlamaLatentNL\% of
not-capability-limited fatal traces);
\item \emph{recoverable errors}: success stays above $\delta$ past the error
(\QwenSmallAllRecovNL\%, \QwenTwoAllRecovNL\%, \QwenMathRecovNL\% and \LlamaRecovNL\%).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Event study.} Aligned at the annotated error (Fig.~\ref{fig:event}), fatal traces show a step
change from $r=-1$ to $r=0$:
\QwenSmallAllEvFatBefore$\to$\QwenSmallAllEvFatAfter,
\QwenTwoAllEvFatBefore$\to$\QwenTwoAllEvFatAfter,
\QwenMathEvFatBefore$\to$\QwenMathEvFatAfter\ and \LlamaEvFatBefore$\to$\LlamaEvFatAfter. Clean
traces aligned at a random step drift smoothly upward, as the success-conditioned analogue of
Eq.~\ref{eq:supermart} predicts. For recovered traces of Qwen2.5-Math-7B, success \emph{rises}
across the annotated error (\QwenMathEvRecBefore$\to$\QwenMathEvRecAfter), by far more than the
drift of clean traces.

\textbf{Does the model already know its answer?} (exploratory) At every prefix we force an
immediate answer (\ProbeSamples\ forced answers), giving an \emph{answer curve} $a_k$. The probe is
valid at the endpoints. After the full trace, the forced answer reproduces the trace's own answer in
79--91\% of cases for three models, but in only 47\% for Qwen2-1.5B, for which the probe is
unreliable. From the bare question it is right in 11--14\% of cases. Answers become
accessible late, mostly after the error position (Fig.~\ref{fig:answers}). In fatal traces, the
model's accessible answer was correct just before the error and wrong just after it in only
\QwenMathAnsFatTurnsWrongAtError\ (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and \LlamaAnsFatTurnsWrongAtError\ (Llama) of
traces. In most fatal traces the forced answer was never right (\QwenMathAnsFatNeverRight\ and
\LlamaAnsFatNeverRight). In recovered traces, the correct answer is already accessible right
after the error in \QwenMathAnsRecFixed\ ($n=\QwenMathAnsRecN$) and \LlamaAnsRecFixed\
($n=\LlamaAnsRecN$) of traces. This is a lower bound on recoveries in which the written error did
not matter, because answers are computed late even in clean traces.

\subsection{Where to backtrack, per attempt}\label{sec:backtrack}
Verifier-guided search resamples a failing trace from some earlier point. We compare four targets on
fatal traces:
\begin{itemize}
\item restarting from scratch ($v_0$);
\item resampling from just before the annotated first error ($v_{e-1}$);
\item resampling from just before the decisive step ($v_{d-1}$);
\item the best prefix in hindsight (an in-sample upper bound).
\end{itemize}
Choosing a step and scoring it on the same continuations would reward noise, so we split every
prefix's continuations at random into halves $A$ and $B$. Half $A$ selects the trace
($v_0^A>\delta$) and its decisive step, and half $B$ scores every target. We average over 50
splits and bootstrap over traces. On a planted-effect simulation in which the first error is the
decisive step by construction, this estimator returns $+0.001$, so it does not create a gap.

\textbf{For the three Qwen models, backtracking to the annotated first error is worse than starting
over.} This was found exploratorily in Run B. Restart beats first-error backtracking by
\QwenSmallAllBtRestErr, \QwenTwoAllBtRestErr\ and \QwenMathBtRestErr\ (Table~\ref{tab:backtrack}).
On the 48 new continuations per prefix alone, which were sampled after the effect was found, it
replicates for both 1.5B models. The pooled difference is \PooledNewBtRestErr\
[\PooledNewBtRestErrLo, \PooledNewBtRestErrHi] (preregistered R1, confirmed). The steps before the
error already lower the chance of success. Resampling from just before the decisive step recovers
the loss: it beats first-error backtracking by \QwenSmallAllBtDiff, \QwenTwoAllBtDiff\ and
\QwenMathBtDiff\ (all CIs above zero).

\textbf{For Llama-3.1-8B, it is not.} The preregistered confirmatory tests on the new model family
fail:
\begin{itemize}
\item restart $-$ first error is \LlamaBtRestErr\ [\LlamaBtRestErrLo, \LlamaBtRestErrHi]
(C1, Holm-adjusted $p$ = \LlamaHolmCOne);
\item decisive step $-$ first error is \LlamaBtDiff\ [\LlamaBtDiffLo, \LlamaBtDiffHi]
(C2, Holm-adjusted $p$ = \LlamaHolmCTwo).
\end{itemize}
This is not an artefact of truncation. With continuations that hit the token limit treated as
missing rather than wrong, restart $-$ first error is \LlamaTruncBtRestErr\
[\LlamaTruncBtRestErrLo, \LlamaTruncBtRestErrHi]. The Llama result is consistent with its
localization result: the annotated error is the decisive step more often (\LlamaDec), so the step
before the error is usually still a good place to resume. Backtracking to human first errors is
therefore model-dependent. It is worse than restarting for three Qwen models, but not in general.

\textbf{Decisive-step backtracking and restart: no difference detected, equivalence mostly
untested.} Decisive $-$ restart is within $\pm0.04$ for every model, and every 95\% CI includes
zero. Equivalence at a margin of $\pm0.05$ (TOST, 90\% CI) holds for Qwen2.5-1.5B and for the
pooled new batches. It is inconclusive for the other models, including the preregistered C4 test
on Llama. We therefore do not claim that decisive-step backtracking matches restart; we claim only
that no difference was detected. It uses 15--29\% fewer continuation tokens per attempt. The best
prefix in hindsight reaches \QwenSmallAllBtOracle--\LlamaBtOracle.

\textbf{Reading.} Eq.~\ref{eq:supermart} explains why restart is a strong baseline: for a
backtracking point at a fixed depth or at a stopping time of the trace, failed on-policy prefixes
are on average no better than the empty prefix. The point just before the first error is neither,
because it depends on the next step. So the inequality allows first-error backtracking to beat
restart or to lose to it. The data show both outcomes, depending on the model.

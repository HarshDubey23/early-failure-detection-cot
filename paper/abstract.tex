\begin{abstract}
Process supervision assumes that the first incorrect step of a reasoning trace is where it fails, and
early-warning detectors assume that failure is visible in a partial trace. We test both assumptions
on 3,400 ProcessBench solutions with expert first-error labels. A detector that reads only the
question and the generator's identity predicts a wrong answer with AUROC \aucQG; a TF-IDF detector
reading the first quarter of the reasoning adds nothing beyond it (\dIncA, n.s.) and is at chance
within a problem. \nonFatalPct\% of annotated first errors are followed by a correct answer
(\nonFatalOmniPct\% on Omni-MATH), fatality does not depend on where the error occurs, and failure
detectors rank these non-fatal errors as riskier than fatal ones (AUROC \twOutFR). We then measure
commitment directly: resampling the generating model from every prefix of its own traces, we locate
the step after which success becomes unlikely and compare it with the annotated first error.
\end{abstract}

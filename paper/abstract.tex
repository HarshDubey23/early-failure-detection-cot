\begin{abstract}
Language models fail mid-generation: a reasoning step goes wrong, or an answer drifts from its
source into hallucination. The standard remedy is surgical: find the step where the failure
happened and repair or regenerate from there. We ask what actually mitigates these failures at the
lowest cost, and answer with one measurement, $v_k$, the generator's own probability of success
from prefix $k$, estimated from \TotalRollouts\ on-policy continuations in mathematics and 11,320 in
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).

\emph{What works is simple.} Detect, restart and, if the output is still flagged, abstain. Plain
restarting recovers \recMinFour--\recMaxFour\% of failed maths solutions within four attempts'
compute. In RAG (RAGTruth, Llama-2-7B), a preregistered detect--resample controller reduces
hallucination relative to the base model (\rrHZero\ [\rrHZeroLo, \rrHZeroHi], $p=\rrHZeroP$, LLM
judge), and the plain detect--restart--abstain loop costs \costDra$\times$ the base model, against
\costCare$\times$ for the choke-point-aware controller and \costCove$\times$ for Chain-of-Verification. We
prove that such a loop delivers answers whose hallucination rate equals the detector's
$P(\text{hallucinated}\mid\text{pass})$, independent of the retry budget; with human labels this
predicts \lawH\% $\to$ \lawPi\% [\lawPiLo, \lawPiHi] at the setting we ran, and a stricter gate is predicted
to reach \ocUnionPi\% at \ocUnionDraws\ generations per answer. In the replay, a four-fold retry budget raises
coverage by \invCov\ points while delivered hallucination moves by only \invETwo\ to \invEOne\ points, as the
law predicts.

\emph{Why the surgical version does not pay.} Early-warning signals mostly read difficulty
(question and model alone: AUROC \aucQG). \QwenSmallAllCapPct--\QwenMathCapPct\% of failures are
decided before the first step, and the human-labelled error is the decisive step only in
\QwenTwoAllDec--\QwenSmallAllDec\ of the rest (preregistered, confirmed on a held-out family). On our
new benchmark, ChokeBench, a process reward model finds \cbPrmDropAccHPct\% of human first errors but
\cbPrmDropAccDPct\% of choke points, and even a free oracle for the choke point gains only
\cbOracleGainB\ at four restarts' budget. A supermartingale bound explains why: a failed prefix is,
on average, worth no more than a fresh start, so locating the failure saves tokens, not successes.
The same holds for hallucinations, where choosing where to resample adds \rrHFive.

Finally, the measured gain of a detector-gated loop depends on the grader: \robBaseETwoDiff\ under a
grader trained like the detector, \robBaseEOneDiff\ (n.s.) under an independent judge. All rollouts,
preregistrations and the code behind every number are released.
\end{abstract}

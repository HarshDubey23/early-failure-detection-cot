\begin{abstract}
Process reward models are trained and benchmarked to find the first incorrect step of a reasoning
trace, and verifier-guided search returns to that step to repair it. This assumes that a failure
is \emph{decided} at the labelled error, and that resampling from just before it beats starting
again. We test both assumptions with one measurement, $v_k$: the generator's own probability of
success from prefix $k$, estimated from \TotalRollouts\ on-policy continuations of human-annotated
failed traces from four generators.

Both assumptions mostly fail. \QwenSmallAllCapPct--\QwenMathCapPct\% of failures are decided
before the first step, and in the rest the human-labelled error is the decisive step only
\QwenTwoAllDec--\QwenSmallAllDec\ of the time, against 0.16--0.20 by chance (preregistered; confirmed on a held-out model
family). On ChokeBench, a benchmark and evaluation protocol we release (\cbN\ failed traces, four
generators), a process reward model finds \cbPrmDropAccHPct\% of human first errors but only
\cbPrmDropAccDPct\% of the decisive steps, and on held-out models ChokeBench scores rank localisers
by their actual recovery gain better than first-error scores do (Spearman \valRhoD\ against \valRhoE). At matched compute, no deployable resampling point reliably beats
plain restart, and even a free oracle for the decisive step gains only \cbOracleGainB\ at four
restarts' budget, while restarting recovers \recMinFour--\recMaxFour\% of failed solutions within
four attempts. A supermartingale bound explains the pattern: a failed on-policy prefix is, in
expectation, worth no more than a fresh start, so localising the failure saves tokens rather than
adding successes. On exactly on-policy traces of a reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B), no cut
of a failed trace is worth more than a fresh start, as the bound predicts, though a gain from cheaper
late attempts could not be ruled out; most of its failures are budget exhaustion, with no wrong step to
find. As a negative result, early-warning signals mostly read problem difficulty: the
question and model alone give AUROC \aucQG, and the first quarter of the reasoning adds \dIncA\ (n.s.).

In a secondary study on retrieval-augmented generation (RAGTruth, Llama-2-7B), a preregistered
detect--resample controller lowers the judged hallucination rate against the base model
(\rrHZero, $p=\rrHZeroP$), and plain restarting does about the same (\vbDraEOne\ [\vbDraEOneLo, \vbDraEOneHi],
exploratory). That judge agrees only weakly with people ($\kappa=\evEOneKappa$), the
choke-point-aware variant is no better than plain restarting (\rrHOne) at higher cost
(\costCare$\times$ against \costDra$\times$ the base model), and outputs have not yet been rated by
people. The measured gain also depends on the grader: \robBaseETwoDiff\ under a grader trained like
the gate's detector, \robBaseEOneDiff\ (n.s.) under an independent judge. Counterfactual resampling
itself is not new; what we add is its comparison with human error labels, verifiers and recovery
policies at matched compute. All rollouts, preregistrations and the code behind every number are
released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Practical Policies at a Matched Budget}\label{sec:policies}
Section~\ref{sec:backtrack} compares targets per attempt, using oracle knowledge of the decisive
step. A deployed system has to choose a restart point without rollouts and pays for every token. We
therefore compare practical policies against restart under a matched end-to-end budget.

\textbf{Policies.} All were fixed before the confirmatory data existed (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}).
\begin{itemize}
\item Restart.
\item PRM first error: resample from before the first step that Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B~\cite{zhang2025lessons}
scores below 0.5.
\item PRM largest drop: resample from before the step with the largest fall in PRM score.
\item Probe search: two probe continuations at each of three prefixes, then resample from the
latest prefix with a success.
\item Learned: logistic regression on step features (PRM score and drop, the generator's NLL and
entropy, position, length), trained to predict the decisive step on the \emph{other} generators.
For Llama, it is trained only on the three Qwen models.
\item Two references: human first error (the expert label, not available in deployment) and the
oracle decisive step (all its rollouts counted).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Implementation check.} Our implementation of the verifier loads the reward head directly
from the checkpoint. It reaches a ProcessBench F1 of \prmFOne\ over \PrmTraces\ traces
(GSM8K \prmFOneGsm, MATH \prmFOneMath, OlympiadBench \prmFOneOlym, Omni-MATH \prmFOneOmni). The
published value is 73.5~\cite{zhang2025lessons}.

\textbf{Cost.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Tokens are weighted by model size relative to the generator (FLOPs $\propto$ parameters
$\times$ tokens).
\item Every prefill (question plus prefix) counts once per restart point, assuming prefix caching.
\item Every generated token counts.
\item Verifier passes, probes and oracle rollouts are charged to the policies that use them.
\item The budget is $b$ times the cost of one restart attempt, for each trace.
\item A policy spends what is left on independent attempts from its restart point. We report the
probability that at least one attempt is correct (Jeffreys-posterior estimate from held-out
continuations, 50 splits, trace bootstrap).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{No practical policy beats restart reliably} (Table~\ref{tab:policies},
Fig.~\ref{fig:policies}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Llama (confirmatory).} PRM largest-drop backtracking is \emph{worse} than restart at
four attempts, \LlamaPolPrmDropFourDiff\ [\LlamaPolPrmDropFourLo, \LlamaPolPrmDropFourHi]. So is the
learned policy transferred from Qwen, at \LlamaPolLearnedFourDiff\ [\LlamaPolLearnedFourLo,
\LlamaPolLearnedFourHi].
\item \textbf{The 1.5B models.} Scoring the trace with a 7B verifier costs about five generator
attempts, so every verifier-based policy loses heavily at small budgets.
\item \textbf{Qwen2.5-Math-7B.} PRM largest-drop backtracking is the only practical policy with a
positive estimate. Under full accounting it is \QwenMathPolPrmDropFourDiff\
[\QwenMathPolPrmDropFourLo, \QwenMathPolPrmDropFourHi] at four attempts and
\QwenMathPolPrmDropEightDiff\ [\QwenMathPolPrmDropEightLo, \QwenMathPolPrmDropEightHi] at eight. If
the verifier pass is assumed to be paid anyway, it is \QwenMathPolPrmDropFourShDiff\
[\QwenMathPolPrmDropFourShLo, \QwenMathPolPrmDropFourShHi] (exploratory).
\item \textbf{Probe search and the oracle.} Both spend most of the budget on finding the restart
point, and lose.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Even a perfect first-error verifier is no shortcut.} The human first error, which no
deployed system has, is within a few points of restart at matched compute for every model:
\QwenSmallPolHumanFourDiff, \QwenTwoPolHumanFourDiff, \QwenMathPolHumanFourDiff\ and
\LlamaPolHumanFourDiff. Later restart points buy more attempts, but each attempt is worth less,
which is the trade-off the proposition in Appendix~\ref{app:prop} anticipates.

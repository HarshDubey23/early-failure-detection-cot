\section{The Same Question for Hallucinations}\label{sec:grounded}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) offers what mathematics lacks: a detector can check, while the
system runs, whether each sentence is supported by the source. If hallucinations were choke points,
a controller that estimates $v_k$ online and resamples from the most recoverable boundary should
beat plain restarting. We built such a controller and preregistered its test
(\texttt{docs/PREREGISTRATION\_RECOVERY.md}, committed before any data).

\textbf{Setup.} RAGTruth~\cite{ragtruth} test sources (450; QA, summarisation, data-to-text), whose
Llama-2-7B-chat responses carry human span labels, and 300 HotpotQA~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa}
items with controlled evidence (half with the answer removed). The gate $D$ is LettuceDetect-base~\cite{lettucedetect}.
When $D$ flags a sentence, the controller (CARE) tries claim repair, then resamples, choosing
between restarting and backtracking to just before the flagged sentence by Thompson sampling on
each boundary's estimated recoverability per token, then abstains (QA) or deletes what is still
flagged. Every policy replays the same bank of on-policy continuations (\mechCont\ in the mechanism
study alone), under a budget cap of $b=2$ draft-equivalents; tokens actually spent are reported.
The preregistered primary evaluator $E_1$ is an LLM judge from another family (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct;
$\kappa=\evEOneKappa$ with RAGTruth's human labels, passing the preregistered bar of 0.20); $E_2$
(LettuceDetect-large) is secondary because it shares training data with $D$.

\textbf{Preregistered results (RAGTruth).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{H0, sanity:} CARE reduces hallucination relative to the base model,
\rrHZero\ [\rrHZeroLo, \rrHZeroHi] ($p=\rrHZeroP$).
\item \emph{H1, versus restart until $D$ passes:} \rrHOne\ [\rrHOneLo, \rrHOneHi]; equivalent within
$\pm$0.03 (TOST 90\% CI [\rrTostLo, \rrTostHi]). Restart spends \rrRestartTok\ tokens per item
against \rrCareTok\ for CARE.
\item \emph{H5, does the commitment-aware choice add anything?} CARE minus the same controller
restricted to restarts: \rrHFive\ [\rrHFiveLo, \rrHFiveHi] under $E_1$ and \rrHFiveEtwo\
[\rrHFiveEtwoLo, \rrHFiveEtwoHi] under $E_2$. An oracle that knows each boundary's true
recoverability gains \rrOracle\ [\rrOracleLo, \rrOracleHi]: there was little to choose between.
\item \emph{Mechanism, with human span labels:} over \mechN\ human-hallucinated drafts, resampling
from just before the first hallucinated sentence is no better than restarting (\mechDiff\
[\mechDiffLo, \mechDiffHi], $p=\mechP$; \mechSum\ for summaries, exploratory). Keeping a
hallucinated sentence does not raise the hazard of later ones relative to a placebo (\mechSnow\
[\mechSnowLo, \mechSnowHi]): no snowballing~\cite{zhang2024snowball}.
\end{itemize}
HotpotQA agrees: H0 holds and no comparison against an alternative strategy is significant.

\textbf{Who grades the loop matters} (exploratory). Detector-gated policies resample until $D$ is
satisfied, so an evaluator that resembles $D$ will tend to agree that they succeeded. We score
every RAGTruth policy on the \robNTest\ sources of a held-out half under five evaluators
(Table~\ref{tab:evaluators}). One of them never reads a LettuceDetect score: $E_1$ with a threshold on
the share of sentences it calls unsupported (\robFstar\%), chosen on the other \robNDev\ drafts from a
grid fixed in advance. Its human agreement is weak ($\kappa=\robKEOneCal$ on the held-out drafts),
like $E_1$'s (\robKEOne); $E_2$, the ``dual'' rule $E_1\wedge E_2{\ge}0.3$ and $D$ agree better
(\robKETwo, \robKDual, \robKD) but share training data with the gate ($E_2$ agrees with $D$ on
\robAgreeETwoD\% of drafts, $E_1$-calibrated on \robAgreeEOneD\%). The threshold 0.3 of the dual rule was
chosen on all drafts, so its held-out $\kappa$ is not an out-of-sample estimate.

Two conclusions survive the change of evaluator: CARE beats restart under none of the five, and
it matches its restart-only ablation under both preregistered evaluators (H5). Everything else depends on the evaluator. Restarting until $D$ passes
lowers the hallucination rate by \robBaseETwoDiff\ according to $E_2$ and \robBaseDualDiff\ according to
the dual rule, but by \robBaseEOneDiff\ [\robBaseEOneDiffLo, \robBaseEOneDiffHi] according to $E_1$ and
\robBaseEOneCalDiff\ [\robBaseEOneCalDiffLo, \robBaseEOneCalDiffHi] according to its calibrated
version. Chain-of-Verification~\cite{dhuliawala2024cove}, which never consults $D$, looks far worse
than restart to the $D$-family (\robCoveDualDiff\ under the dual rule) and indistinguishable from it
to the independent judges (\robCoveEOneDiff\ [\robCoveEOneDiffLo, \robCoveEOneDiffHi]). The independent
judges are weak, so they cannot rule out a real reduction; but the $D$-family numbers are partly
the gate grading itself. Gains of a verifier loop should be measured by an evaluator that shares
no training data with the verifier, and ultimately by people (a blinded two-rater kit is
released; Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\textbf{Reading.} As in mathematics, the step where the output first goes wrong is a fine but not a
better place to resume than the beginning, and an online estimate of recoverability does not
change that. Hallucinations here behave like local errors rather than commitments:
detecting and resampling them reduces hallucination by an amount the evaluators disagree on, and
choosing where to resample adds nothing.

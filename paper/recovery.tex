\section{Generalization to Grounded Generation}\label{sec:grounded}
Does the finding that errors are not choke points transfer beyond symbolic mathematics to open-domain text generation? In retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), a prominent hypothesis holds that when a model begins hallucinating unsupported claims, early detection of the hallucination onset enables surgical recovery---either backtracking to the first faithful prefix, revising unsupported claims, or dynamically switching recovery actions based on estimated recoverability~\cite{shuster2021,dhuliawala2024cove,gao2023rarr}.

We tested this hypothesis directly on RAGTruth~\cite{ragtruth} (450 sources across QA, summarization, and data-to-text) and HotpotQA~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa} (300 items with controlled evidence), using Llama-2-7B-chat and Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (1,500 total generation items).

\subsection{Evaluator calibration and out-of-sample validation}
Evaluating hallucination recovery requires an accurate judge. An uncalibrated zero-shot LLM judge ($E_1$: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) flags \judgeEOneRate\% of drafts as hallucinated, where human annotators find only 50.3\%, yielding poor human agreement ($\kappa=\judgeEOneKappa$). This over-flagging inflates baseline error rates and obscures true recovery dynamics.

To address this evaluation bottleneck, we constructed a \emph{Dual Consensus Judge} ($E_{\text{dual}} = E_1 \land E_2[\tau \ge 0.3]$), requiring that an LLM-detected reasoning hallucination be corroborated by token-level support divergence from a ModernBERT groundedness classifier ($E_2$: LettuceDetect-Large)~\cite{lettucedetect}. To guard against post-hoc overfitting, we calibrated the threshold $\tau=0.30$ on a 50\% development split of sources ($n=217$, $\kappa=\judgeDualDevKappa$) and evaluated the frozen decision rule on an untouched, held-out test split ($n=232$). On the untouched test set, the frozen judge achieves $\kappa=\judgeDualTestKappa$, accuracy \judgeDualTestAcc\%, and precision \judgeDualTestPrec\% (matching the ground-truth rate of 51.3\%), compared to $\kappa=\judgeEOneTestKappa$ for the zero-shot LLM judge. Pooled across all 449 drafts, the dual judge reaches $\kappa=\judgeDualPooledKappa$ (Table~\ref{tab:grounded_eval}).

\subsection{Policy comparison: restart dominates commitment-aware recovery}
We evaluated six executed recovery policies under the calibrated judge at matched compute budget ($b=2$, at most two calls per completion) on RAGTruth test (Table~\ref{tab:grounded_policies}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Raw baseline}: Zero-shot draft without intervention (\ragRawHall\% hallucination, \ragRawFaith\% faithful answers delivered).
\item \textbf{Plain restart ($b=2$)}: When detector $D$ flags a draft, discard and resample from scratch.
\item \textbf{CARE controller}: Commitment-Aware Recovery Engine dynamically selects between claim editing, prefix backtracking, and restarting based on local recoverability estimates.
\item \textbf{Task-aware policy}: Selective abstention on flagged QA drafts; restart on synthesis tasks.
\item \textbf{Chain-of-Verification (CoVe)}: Factored generate-and-revise baseline~\cite{dhuliawala2024cove}.
\end{itemize}

The empirical outcome exactly mirrors our findings in mathematical reasoning:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The commitment-aware controller fails to beat restart.} CARE delivers fewer faithful answers than plain restart (\ragCareFaith\% vs \ragRestartFaith\%) and has a higher hallucination rate (\ragCareHall\% vs \ragRestartHall\%) while costing 29\% more tokens (\ragCareCost\ vs \ragRestartCost).
\item \textbf{Surgical backtracking is no shortcut.} Resampling from just before the first human-labelled hallucinated sentence is no better than restarting (+0.024 [$-0.002, +0.049$], $p=0.066$).
\item \textbf{No error snowballing.} Keeping an unfaithful sentence does not increase the hallucination hazard of subsequent sentences ($-0.006$ [$-0.029, +0.019$]). Hallucinations behave as localized generation slips, not irrecoverable path commitments.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The safety--utility trade-off}
The Task-Aware policy achieves the lowest apparent hallucination rate (\ragTaskHall\%). However, it achieves this strictly by refusing to answer flagged QA prompts. This reduces answer coverage to \ragTaskCov\% and drops total faithful answers delivered to \ragTaskFaith\% (compared to \ragRestartFaith\% for plain restart). While selective abstention represents a valid safety preference in zero-tolerance environments, it does not represent Pareto dominance: plain restart remains the dominant strategy for maximizing faithful utility.

\subsection{Cross-architecture generalization}
Evaluating 1,500 outputs across Llama-2-7B and Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct shows that architectural scaling substantially reduces overall failure flags (\crossLlamaTwoFlag\% $\rightarrow$ \crossLlamaThreeFlag\%). On short-form QA, instruction tuning reduces failures from \crossLlamaTwoQa\% to \crossLlamaThreeQa\%. However, complex synthesis tasks continue to experience frequent failure flags (\crossLlamaThreeData\% for data-to-text, \crossLlamaThreeSummary\% for summarization), confirming that early failure detection remains an active challenge for long-form reasoning and synthesis.

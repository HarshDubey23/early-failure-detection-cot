\begin{abstract}
Process supervision assumes that the first incorrect step of a reasoning trace is where it fails;
verifier-guided search backtracks to that step, and early-warning detectors assume failure is visible
in a partial trace. We test these assumptions on 3,400 ProcessBench solutions with expert first-error
labels. A detector that reads only the question and the generator's identity predicts a wrong answer
with AUROC \aucQG; a TF-IDF detector reading the first quarter of the reasoning adds nothing beyond it
(\dIncA, n.s.) and is at chance within a problem. \nonFatalPct\% of annotated first errors are
followed by a correct answer, fatality does not depend on where the error occurs, and failure
detectors rank these non-fatal errors as riskier than fatal ones (AUROC \twOutFR). We then measure
commitment counterfactually, scoring \TotalRollouts\ continuations sampled from three open models at
every prefix of their own traces. \QwenSmallCapPct--\QwenMathCapPct\% of failures are decided before
the first step (the model almost never solves the problem). When the outcome is still open, the
annotated error is the largest drop in success probability in \QwenTwoDec--\QwenSmallDec\ of traces
(position-matched null \QwenMathDecNull--\QwenTwoDecNull). Most strikingly, resampling from just
before the expert-labelled first error succeeds less often than restarting from scratch in all three
models (by \QwenMathBtRestErr\ to \QwenTwoBtRestErr), whereas resampling from where success actually
drops matches restarting with \QwenTwoTokSave--\QwenMathTokSave\% fewer tokens. Verifiers that steer
search should target drops in success probability, not first errors.
\end{abstract}
